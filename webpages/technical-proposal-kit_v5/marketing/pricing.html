Why these three tiers

The logic behind the ladder

Starter exists to remove the reason not to start. A contractor who bids three times a year will not pay professional prices, but they will pay the price of one printed form set to stop rebuilding the same cover letter from scratch. The seat limit and the single language keep it from cannibalising Professional without making it feel punitive.

Professional is the intended destination. The Arabic edition, the Excel workbook and your own logo on the forms are the three things a firm bidding continuously actually needs, and all three are invisible to a first-time buyer — which is why they are grouped rather than sold separately. Roughly two-thirds of revenue in a ladder like this should land on the middle tier; if it does not, either the middle is priced too close to the top or the top is not delivering enough.

Enterprise is priced for procurement, not for individuals. Custom forms, a rate library and a named manager are services, and services are bought by committee. The four-hour response commitment is the line that closes these deals — it is the only item on the page a branch manager cannot get internally.

Monthly versus annual

Annual billing at ten months' price is a 17% discount, which is generous but deliberate: it converts a subscription into a budget line, and a budget line survives the month a firm does not bid. Churn in contractor tools is seasonal — bids cluster around tender cycles — and annual billing is the only instrument that smooths it.

What is deliberately not metered

Projects, prints and downloads are unlimited on every paid tier. Metering those creates a reason to avoid using the product on the tender that matters most, which is precisely the tender that decides whether the subscription renews.

Positioning

This is priced as a proposal-production tool, not as estimating software. Estimating platforms cost ten times more and are bought by a different person. At these prices a single avoided administrative rejection pays for several years.

Questions

Before you choose

Is this a subscription or a one-off purchase?

Subscription. The forms are maintained — code editions change, currencies move, forms get corrected — and a subscription is what funds that maintenance.

Can I cancel?

Yes, at any time. Access continues to the end of the paid period. Anything you have already printed or exported as PDF remains yours.

Do the forms work offline?

Yes. They are HTML files that run in the browser with no server dependency. Autosave uses local browser storage, so your values stay on the machine you typed them on.

What happens to my data?

Nothing leaves your browser. There is no account, no upload and no analytics on the form pages. This is deliberate: tender pricing is commercially sensitive.
