The problem

Most lost tenders are lost before the price is opened

Technical evaluation is a scoring exercise with a gate in front of it. Contractors spend weeks on the price and an afternoon on the submission — then discover the gate was the whole competition.

⚠
Administrative rejection

An expired certificate, a missing bond reference, an unsigned page. The technical score never gets read, and nobody tells you why.

📈
Numbers that do not reconcile

The cover letter says one figure, the BOQ another, the payment schedule implies a third. The evaluator queries it, and confidence in the whole offer drops.

📄
Generic methodology

The same twelve pages submitted to every tender. Evaluators score it against the actual scope, and a document that could describe any project describes none.

The solution

One kit, thirteen documents, one set of numbers

The quantity take-off feeds the price schedule; the price schedule feeds the payment ladder; the payment ladder has to be defensible from the BOQ groups. The kit is built so those links are arithmetic, not promises — and so the last review catches the inconsistency before the client does.

Questions

The things contractors ask first

Do I need Excel, or is the browser enough?

Either. The HTML forms calculate in the browser and print directly to PDF. The Excel workbook is for firms who want to keep the take-off as a working file and update dimensions later without reprinting the page.

Is the pricing in the examples real?

No, and the document says so on the page. The unit rates in the price schedule are illustrative — they exist to show the arithmetic from take-off to offer value. They must be replaced with live supplier quotations before submission. The quantities are derived from declared geometry and can be checked by hand.

Can I put my own logo on it?

On Professional and Enterprise. Starter prints with the kit's own header so that branding remains the reason to move up a tier.

Does it work for Arabic submissions?

Yes — the Arabic edition is a full RTL version of all thirteen documents, with the same calculations and the same cross-checks. It is included in Professional and Enterprise.

What if a code changes and a form becomes out of date?

Forms are versioned and corrected; corrections are part of what the subscription pays for. Enterprise subscribers also receive the quarterly code-change brief.
