2 Overall Execution Methodology

We execute the Works on a zone-by-zone, floor-by-floor basis in which each zone passes through the same four gates before the next trade enters it: setting-out release, structural sign-off, services first-fix completion, and finishing release. This keeps trades from overtaking each other and gives the supervision team a single, repeatable checkpoint per zone rather than a project-wide free-for-all.

  1. Mobilisation and setting-out release

    Site establishment, temporary facilities, survey control transfer from the Employer's benchmarks, and a joint setting-out check signed by the site engineer and the consultant before any excavation begins. No excavation starts on an unsigned setting-out sheet.

  2. Substructure sequence

    Excavate and blind in panels, not the full footprint, so that reinforcement and formwork crews work continuously rather than in stop-start waves. Each panel is released for concreting only against a signed reinforcement inspection and a concrete pour card.

  3. Superstructure cycle

    A fixed seven-day cycle per floor: day 1 column steel, day 2 column shutters and pour, day 3–4 beam and slab soffit, day 5 beam and slab steel, day 6 services sleeves and embedments, day 7 slab pour. The cycle is what makes the programme credible; it is measured weekly and reported as a variance.

  4. Envelope and weathertightness

    Envelope closes zone by zone so that internal finishes follow behind a dry line rather than waiting for the whole building. Waterproofing is flood-tested before screed, and the test record is a hold point.

  5. Services first and second fix

    Coordinated services drawings are produced from the tender documents before any first fix. Sleeves and box-outs go in with the structure; chasing of completed concrete is prohibited without written approval, because it is the single most common cause of latent structural complaints.

  6. Finishes and handover

    Finishes follow a mock-up first rule: one room or one typical bay of every finish type is completed, inspected and approved as the benchmark before the trade is released to the wider area.

3 Methodology by Principal Work Package

3.1 Earthworks and foundations

Excavation to the depths shown, with sides battered or supported according to the soil report and the adjacent-structure survey. Dewatering is by perimeter sumps and pumps discharging to a silt trap before any connection to the storm network. Backfill is placed in 200 mm layers, each compacted and tested for field density at the frequency stated in the specification; a layer that has not passed is not covered.

3.2 Concrete works

Concrete is supplied from a ready-mix plant with an approved mix design per strength class. Every delivery is checked for slump, temperature and age before discharge, and cubes are taken at the specified frequency with a register that ties each cube set to a pour reference and a structural element. Formwork is designed for the pour pressure actually expected, not by rule of thumb, and struck only when the cube results or the maturity record permit it.

3.3 Reinforcement

Bar bending schedules are produced from the structural drawings and issued to the supplier; the site checks delivered bar against a cutting list, not against the drawing. Cover is controlled with approved spacers at the specified density — cover failures are a durability defect, not a cosmetic one, and they are almost always a spacer-density failure.

3.4 Masonry and blockwork

Blockwork is set out from the structural grid with movement joints at the locations shown. Lintels and bearing details follow the structural drawings. Wall chases are cut by machine to a fixed depth, never by hammer, and no chase runs horizontally across a wall for more than the permitted length.

3.5 Finishes

Plaster is applied to a mechanically prepared background with a bonding agent where the substrate requires it, and checked for flatness with a 2 m straightedge at the stated tolerance. Painting follows the full specified system — primer, intermediate and finish coats — with the dry film thickness verified at each coat; a two-coat job described as three is a defect that shows up within two seasons.

3.6 Electrical

Containment is installed before cable, cable before accessories, and accessories before energisation. Every circuit is identified at both ends and recorded in the cable schedule; insulation resistance and continuity tests are recorded per circuit, and the board schedule is issued as a document, not left as a handwritten card inside the panel.

3.7 Mechanical, plumbing and HVAC

Pipework is pressure-tested in sections before concealment, with the test pressure, duration and drop recorded on a signed test sheet. Drainage is air-tested and then flow-tested. Ductwork is leak-tested to the class stated, and air balancing is carried out by an independent testing and balancing agency whose report is the acceptance document for the HVAC system.

3.8 Fire protection

Sprinkler and standpipe pipework is hydrostatically tested and flushed before connection to the riser. Fire alarm loops are tested device by device against the cause-and-effect matrix, and the matrix itself is verified with the fire consultant before the demonstration to the civil defence authority.

3.9 External works

Levels are set from the finished floor level of the building, not from the road, so that the building drains away from itself. Paving falls, kerb lines and the tie-in to the public network are surveyed and signed off before the final surface course is placed.

4 Coordination and Clash Management

Services coordination is a scheduled activity with its own deliverables, not something that happens when a pipe hits a beam:

  • A combined services model or, at minimum, coordinated overlays at 1:50 for plant rooms, risers, corridors and ceiling voids — the four places where clashes actually occur.
  • Clash reports issued weekly, each with an owner, a decision and a closing date. An open clash older than fourteen days is escalated to the project manager in writing.
  • Penetrations and sleeves agreed with the structural engineer before the pour, never after.
  • Ceiling void depth reserved for services at the design stage and protected during installation; ceiling levels are set after the services are installed, not before.
