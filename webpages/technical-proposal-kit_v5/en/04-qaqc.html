Quality Assurance and Quality Control Plan (QA/QC)

Who owns quality, which inspections are hold points, what happens when work fails, and how records prove it afterwards.

What evaluators look for: not a policy statement, but a named quality engineer, a table of inspection and test points with the inspection authority marked, and a non-conformance procedure that has teeth. A plan without hold points is a hope, not a plan.

1 Quality Policy and Objectives

We deliver the Works to the specification, first time, and we prove it with records. The quality system on this project is a project-specific plan subordinate to our certified quality management system and is auditable against it. Conformance is verified by inspection and test before work is covered, not by retrospective argument after it is rejected.

4 Material and Procurement Control

  • Submittals before order: material data sheets, certificates of conformity, samples and, where specified, independent test certificates are submitted for approval before any order is placed.
  • Approved brands only: substitution requires written approval and a documented equivalence argument; an unapproved substitute installed is removed at our cost.
  • Delivery inspection: every delivery is checked against the approved submittal and the delivery note, with damaged or non-conforming material segregated and tagged.
  • Storage: cement and bagged materials off the ground and under cover; reinforcement on bearers; pipes capped; cable on drums off the ground. Storage damage is our risk, not a claim.
  • Traceability: heat numbers on structural steel and batch numbers on concrete are recorded against the element they went into.

5 Non-Conformance (NCR) and Corrective Action

  1. Identify and segregate

    Any non-conforming work is tagged and, where it affects safety or durability, stopped immediately by the QA/QC engineer. The tag stays until the disposition is closed.

  2. Raise the NCR

    NCR number, date, location, description, specification clause breached, and the immediate containment action. Photographs are attached; the NCR is issued within 24 hours of identification.

  3. Disposition

    One of: rework to specification, repair by an approved method, accept with the consultant's written concession, or reject and remove. Structural dispositions require the structural engineer's written agreement — the contractor cannot self-accept a structural deviation.

  4. Root cause

    Stated in one sentence naming the process failure, not the person. "Spacer density not specified in the method statement" is a root cause; "operator error" is not.

  5. Corrective action

    A change to the method statement, the checklist or the training record that prevents recurrence, with an owner and a date.

  6. Verify and close

    Re-inspection confirms the fix; the closure is signed and the NCR log updated. An NCR closed without re-inspection is not closed.

6 Document and Record Control

Every record carries a unique number of the form PROJECT-DISC-SEQ-REV and is filed against the element it concerns, so that a future query about one column can be answered without searching the project. Records are retained for the period stated in the contract and handed over as part of the closeout document set: inspection records, test certificates, cube results, NCR log, material approvals and commissioning reports.
