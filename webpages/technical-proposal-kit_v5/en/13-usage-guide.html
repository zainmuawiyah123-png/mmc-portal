How to Use the Kit: Filling the Forms, Pricing, and Reviewing Before Submission

The order of work matters as much as the content. Fill in this sequence and the documents will agree with each other.

Read this page first. The kit is not thirteen independent forms; it is one offer expressed thirteen ways. The quantities flow from TP-14 into TP-09, the payment percentages in TP-03 follow from the BOQ groups, and the methodology in TP-02 must match both. Fill them out of order and they will contradict each other in front of the evaluator.

2 The Order of Work

  1. Read the tender documents completely, once, before writing anything

    Instructions, conditions, specification, drawings, BOQ and every addendum. Then read the drawing register and confirm which revisions are current. An offer built on a superseded drawing is worthless however well written.

  2. Complete TP-10, the tender checklist

    This tells you what must be produced and what must be requested from other people — certificates, references, audited accounts. Those take days; start them now.

  3. Measure, using TP-G1 and TP-14

    Every quantity traced to a drawing number, revision and dimension. Do not price anything until the take-off is complete, or you will price the same item twice.

  4. Price in TP-09

    Enter unit rates, apply waste and the overhead and profit factors. The offer value emerges from the arithmetic — do not decide the offer value first and back-fill rates.

  5. Write TP-02, then TP-04 and TP-05

    The methodology must describe the project you have just measured. Specificity here is what separates a scored submission from a rejected one.

  6. Fill TP-06, TP-07 and TP-08

    Names, plant, sources, references and risks. Each must be verifiable, and none may be repeated from another tender without checking it is still true.

  7. Build TP-03 from the priced BOQ

    Payment percentages must follow the value of the work, not a preferred cash flow. If the programme and the payment ladder disagree, the evaluator sees it immediately.

  8. Complete TP-12 (deviations) and TP-11 (any outstanding RFIs)

    Every departure recorded before submission; anything unresolved becomes your risk at the price you bid.

  9. Write TP-01 last and sign everything

    The cover letter summarises an offer that now exists. Check that the figure in the letter matches TP-09 to the last digit.

The single most common internal inconsistency

A payment schedule written before the BOQ was priced. It always over-weights early milestones, because mobilisation feels expensive when you are staring at an empty site. It is not: on a building project, mobilisation is typically 3–5% of the contract value. If TP-03 claims 15% at mobilisation, the evaluator will query it, and if it is accepted the employer will query it again at the first payment certificate.

4 Filling the Forms in a Browser

  • Type directly into the page. Every field is editable and saves to your own browser as you type — close the tab and come back, the values are still there. They are not sent anywhere.
  • Print or save as PDF with the button in the top bar. Use your browser's print dialog, A4, and enable background graphics so the shaded tables print correctly.
  • Clear wipes the saved values for the current document only, after a confirmation prompt. Use it when you start a new tender.
  • Currency switches every money figure between USD, JOD, SAR, AED, GBP and EUR at fixed reference rates. Edit the rates in assets/en.js if you need live rates — the figures are converted at display time, so nothing you typed is destroyed.
  • Never print to a scanned image and then fill by hand; it defeats the calculation and loses the record trail.

7 What to Change Before You Submit

Mandatory substitutions

Every unit rate in TP-09 is illustrative. It exists to demonstrate the arithmetic of the chain from take-off to offer value, and it is not a market price. Before submission, replace every rate with a live supplier or subcontract quotation, and replace the overhead and profit factors with your own company figures. The quantities may be used as a worked example; the prices may not.

Checklist for adapting the example

  • Replace the project name, client, tender number and dates on TP-01.
  • Replace the programme durations on TP-03 with your own planned durations.
  • Replace every CV, project name, client and reference on TP-06 and TP-07 with real, verifiable ones.
  • Replace the risk register entries on TP-08 with risks specific to the actual site and scope.
  • Replace all 24 rates on TP-09 and the overhead and profit factors.
  • Adjust TP-14 dimensions to the actual drawings, then re-check that TP-14 and TP-09 still agree.
